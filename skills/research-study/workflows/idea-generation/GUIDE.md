# Idea Generation

Move from broad possibilities to one or more evidence-backed, testable ideas. Novelty must be searched, not asserted from memory.

## Context

Capture the field, user's interests and skills, available data/tools, compute or budget, timeframe, ethical constraints, and desired contribution type. Use supplied papers or route through [literature-review](../literature-review/GUIDE.md) when novelty or field coverage matters.

## Stage 1: Diverge

Generate a varied candidate set using several lenses rather than small wording variants:

- problem-first and stakeholder pain;
- failure or boundary-case analysis;
- contradiction between findings or assumptions;
- changed technical, regulatory, social, or data conditions;
- cross-domain transfer;
- simplification or removal of an assumption;
- decomposition or recombination;
- new measurement, dataset, benchmark, replication, or negative result.

For each candidate, state the problem, hypothesized insight, expected contribution, minimum credible test, main risk, and why it may matter.

## Stage 2: Challenge and Search

Shortlist candidates before expensive searching. For each shortlisted idea:

- search direct synonyms, adjacent terminology, and likely prior approaches;
- identify the closest work and the actual difference;
- test whether the difference is meaningful rather than cosmetic;
- look for feasibility blockers, missing data, ethical concerns, and obvious counterexamples;
- assign novelty confidence based on the searched corpus.

Reject or merge duplicates. Record ideas that are valuable but infeasible now in an idea bank rather than presenting them as recommendations.

## Stage 3: Converge

Rank remaining ideas by importance, novelty evidence, technical feasibility, evaluation clarity, resource fit, risk, time to first informative result, and fit with the user's goals. Explain why the top choice wins and what evidence could reverse the ranking.

Use [idea-proposal.md](templates/idea-proposal.md) when the user wants a decision-ready concept.

## Output Contract

Return a candidate table, closest-work check for the shortlist, scoring rationale, selected idea(s), minimum viable study, risks and mitigations, and next validation steps. Do not label an idea “novel” without describing the search boundary and confidence.
