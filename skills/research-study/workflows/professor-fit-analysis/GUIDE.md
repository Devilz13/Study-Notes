# Professor Fit Analysis

Build an evidence-backed fit assessment rather than a prestige ranking. Current information requires live research from primary or authoritative sources.

## Inputs

Ask for the professor/lab and institution plus the user's level, research interests, relevant skills, intended degree/project, geographic or funding constraints, and desired output. Resolve names and affiliations before analysis.

## Evidence Collection

Prefer official faculty/lab pages, verified publication profiles, papers, grant/project pages, institutional directories, and field-specific bibliographic databases. Use conference bios, interviews, or professional profiles only for claims they directly support. Do not treat LinkedIn descriptions, citation counts, or lab marketing as proof of mentoring quality.

Record source date and separate current facts, historical facts, and inference.

## Analysis

1. **Field profile:** identify the discipline's publication, authorship, funding, and collaboration norms.
2. **Research trajectory:** map recent themes, methods, venues, collaborators, and changes over time.
3. **Contribution sample:** read representative recent and foundational papers rather than judging from titles.
4. **Resource signals:** identify verified datasets, systems, grants, facilities, collaborations, and student opportunities.
5. **Mentoring evidence:** use public student outcomes, coauthorship patterns, lab policies, and direct statements cautiously; label unknowns.
6. **Applicant fit:** compare topic, method, skills, stage, independence, resource needs, and career goals.
7. **Risks and questions:** surface evidence-backed mismatches and convert uncertain culture/workload questions into interview questions.
8. **Outreach:** if requested, draft a concise message grounded in specific work and a plausible fit; do not fabricate familiarity.

## Guardrails

- Do not infer personality, health, family status, nationality, religion, politics, or other sensitive/private traits.
- Do not equate famous venues or citation counts with good supervision.
- Do not present absence of public evidence as a negative fact.
- Distinguish lab-level resources from resources available to the user's intended role.
- Make funding and open-position claims only from current sources or label them unverified.

## Output Contract

Provide an evidence table, research taxonomy/trajectory, fit and mismatch analysis, confidence and unknowns, targeted questions, and a calibrated verdict. Add outreach or interview material only when requested.
