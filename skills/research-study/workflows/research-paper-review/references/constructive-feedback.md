# Constructive Review Feedback

Every consequential criticism should answer four questions:

1. Where is the issue?
2. What exactly is wrong, unsupported, or unclear?
3. Why does it matter for a claim or decision?
4. What evidence, clarification, or revision could address it?

Prefer neutral formulations such as:

- “The current evidence does not isolate X from Y because …”
- “It is unclear whether …; clarifying this would affect …”
- “The paper would be stronger if …”
- “This claim should be narrowed to … unless the authors can provide …”

Avoid judgments about author competence, motives, effort, identity, or prestige. Do not inflate minor stylistic issues into soundness concerns.

## Major Versus Minor

- **Major:** can overturn or substantially narrow a central claim, contribution, or venue-level decision.
- **Minor:** improves accuracy, reproducibility, presentation, or completeness without changing the central conclusion.

An expensive requested experiment is not automatically major. Explain which uncertainty it resolves and whether a smaller diagnostic could answer the same question.

## Questions for Authors

A useful question has a decision consequence. State the possible answers and how they would affect the assessment when that is not obvious. Do not disguise accusations as questions.
