# Scoring and Decisions

Use the official venue scale and definitions whenever available. Reproduce the scale in the review notes before assigning a value.

## Calibration

- Score the submitted paper under the stated paper type, not an imagined future version.
- Do not average dimension scores mechanically. A single fatal soundness issue can dominate; a minor presentation issue should not.
- Keep written strengths/weaknesses, dimension scores, overall score, and recommendation mutually consistent.
- Base confidence on subfield familiarity, completeness of the paper/artifacts, search coverage, and verification depth.
- Do not use remembered venue acceptance rates as a substitute for evaluation criteria.

## Generic Fallback

Only when no venue scale exists and the user explicitly wants scoring, use:

| Overall | Meaning |
| --- | --- |
| Strong accept | Exceptional, clearly above the relevant standard, with no decision-critical unresolved concern. |
| Accept | Solidly meets the relevant standard; remaining issues are repairable without changing the central claim. |
| Borderline / weak accept | Meaningful strengths and credible core, but one or more material uncertainties remain. |
| Borderline / weak reject | Potentially valuable, but the present evidence or framing falls below the relevant standard. |
| Reject | Central support, novelty, fit, or correctness is insufficient in the submitted version. |
| Strong reject | Fundamental invalidity, mismatch, or absence of a reviewable contribution. Use sparingly. |

## Confidence

Use low, medium, or high unless a venue requires a numeric scale. Explain the limiting factor. Do not claim expert-level confidence solely because the prose is fluent.
