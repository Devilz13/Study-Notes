# Numerical and Consistency Checks

Apply the relevant checks systematically and record exact locations. Do not report “checked” when the evidence was unavailable.

## Claims and Numbers

- Abstract, introduction, results, discussion, and conclusion state the same central claims.
- Values in prose match tables, figures, captions, appendices, and supplementary material.
- Percent improvement is recomputed from named baseline and proposed values.
- Absolute change, relative change, and percentage points are correctly distinguished.
- Totals, subtotals, averages, weighted averages, ratios, and percentages reconcile.
- Units, scales, signs, decimal places, and rounding are consistent.
- Best/second-best formatting matches the actual metric direction and values.

## Statistics and Study Design

- Sample sizes, exclusions, missing data, train/validation/test splits, and denominators agree throughout.
- Number of runs/seeds and error-bar definitions are stated and consistent.
- Reported p-values, confidence/credible intervals, test statistics, degrees of freedom, and effect sizes are mutually plausible.
- Paired versus independent analyses match the design.
- Multiple comparisons, subgroup analyses, and exploratory analyses are identified.
- Claims of equivalence, non-inferiority, no difference, or causality use appropriate evidence.
- Hyperparameter selection and baseline tuning do not use test data or unequal budgets.

## Tables, Figures, and Equations

- Every table/figure/equation is cited, numbered, and described accurately.
- Axes, units, legends, uncertainty, sample sizes, and metric direction are clear.
- Figure text remains legible at the intended page size.
- Symbols are defined, domains and assumptions are clear, and notation is consistent.
- Referenced equations support the stated derivation; no equality or inequality direction silently changes.

## References and Structure

- Internal references resolve to the intended section, figure, table, equation, appendix, and algorithm.
- Citations exist, support the associated claim, and use consistent metadata/style.
- All bibliography entries are cited and all citations have entries, unless the venue permits otherwise.
- Acronyms are defined on first substantive use and are used consistently.
- The same concept, dataset, method, and metric retain the same name.
- Version, year, venue, dataset release, and code/artifact links agree.
